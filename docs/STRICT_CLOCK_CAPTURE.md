# Planned clock capture

Both native repositories independently recognize entire clock phrases before validation. Twelve-hour input uses 1–12 with AM/PM; 24-hour input uses 0–23 with two minute digits. Minutes stay within 0–59. `12am` is `00:00`; `12pm` and `noon` are `12:00`; `midnight` is `00:00`. `at 10` retains Taskfold's 10:00 meaning. Existing dot-separated numeric quick-entry clocks remain supported. Saved filters retain their separately documented strict clock grammar; numeric quick-entry validation uses that existing canonical validator after normalizing the supported aliases. This adds no saved-filter predicate or document capability.

Examples: `Call tomorrow at noon`, `Deploy tomorrow at midnight`, `Review every day at noon !30mb`. Time-only capture preserves an existing valid planned date and independent deadline; without a plan it uses the current planning calendar's day. Midnight means the start of that selected day, not its end. Taskfold retains its existing same-day time-only behavior even when the clock has passed; this is not full Todoist time-only rollover parity. Accepted chips preview the chosen clock before Save. Declining a time keeps its exact phrase literal, while an independently accepted date remains. Quotes, escaped clock phrases, URLs and word boundaries stay protected.

Malformed complete clocks such as `13pm`, `0am`, `24:00`, `9:60` and `at 12:00:30` remain literal with feedback; no partial suffix becomes a clock or plan. Multiple explicit clocks remain literal and ask for one choice. An explicit clock plus an elapsed `in 2 hours`/`in 30 minutes` phrase is also ambiguous; neither can overwrite the other while its chip still shows an earlier value. A single elapsed phrase retains the existing elapsed-time behavior. Declined groups are excluded from that ambiguity check. Independent priorities, estimates, deadlines and supported dates remain available.

Cursor-local planned-time suggestions include `at noon` and `at midnight` and use the actual capture parser. Reminder shortcuts accept `!tomorrow noon`, `!midnight` and `!every day noon`; their clock validation uses the same canonical minute. They retain independent reminder dates/schedules and do not create a task plan. Source-zone/DST resolution, future-only absolute reminders, recurring reminder anchors and source-ID/semantic duplicate handling retain their existing implementation. Persisted fields remain canonical `HH:mm` and existing reminder specifications; native queues, export, restore, hourly planning and widgets need no new schema or sibling source dependency.

The natural-language planning comparison is [Todoist's official date/time guide](https://www.todoist.com/help/todoist/features/schedule-a-date-and-time-for-your-todoist-tasks-q7VobO), checked 7 October 2026 (updated 28 August). It documents ordinary AM/PM and colon clocks, natural clock periods and time-only rollover. Noon/midnight are explicit Taskfold aliases in this checkpoint; do not infer complete Todoist grammar parity from them. Natural morning/evening phrases, richer dates/repeat rules, configurable next-week/weekend settings, hierarchy/collaboration filters and other recorded P0 work remain open.
